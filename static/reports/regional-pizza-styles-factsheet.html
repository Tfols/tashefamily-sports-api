Vault Candidates
Three pilgrimage-worthy finds flagged explicitly in the brief — the places where visiting isn't just trying a style, it's standing where the style was actually invented.
Papa's Tomato Pies
Trenton / Robbinsville, NJ · est. 1912
Opened by 17-year-old Neapolitan immigrant Giuseppe "Joe" Papa — cited as the oldest continuously operating family-owned pizza restaurant in the country. The related De Lorenzo's lineage (1947) is a few miles away. Both have since relocated from Trenton proper to nearby Robbinsville.
Beau Jo's
Idaho Springs, CO · est. 1973
Chip Bair invented the Colorado "mountain pie" — thick, hand-braided crust, sold by the pound, honey on the side — right here. Now six Colorado locations, but Idaho Springs is the original.
Frank Pepe's & Sally's Apizza
Wooster Street, New Haven, CT · 1925 / 1938
Already a household favorite in style — this is the upgrade: the two actual originating establishments, a few blocks apart on the same street, both still coal-fired 80–100 years later.
The Erie File
Case File: "Erie-Style" PizzaStatus: No Independent Match
NOT FOUND
What we went looking for

No good, independent sourcing establishes "Erie style" as its own codified pizza tradition. Erie shares in the broader Western New York / Rust Belt cup-and-char pepperoni tradition centered on Buffalo — small-diameter pepperoni cups that curl and char, documented back to WWII-era shops like Bocce Club (1946) and tied to Buffalo's Battistoni Italian Specialty Meats (est. 1931). Erie (like Cleveland and Detroit) never fully switched to "lay-flat" pepperoni, and local shops like Lucky Man's Pizzeria are noted for it — but no dedicated historical writeup treats "Erie style" as its own named tradition the way Old Forge, Quad Cities, or Brier Hill are documented. This is a real gap, not a confident negative: a strong local source may simply not have surfaced.

REAL FIND
What Erie is uniquely documented for

Pepperoni balls — a fried, dough-wrapped pepperoni snack, not a pizza. Originated in Erie's Little Italy, reportedly evolving from Depression-era fried fish-scrap balls; the Mohican Market and Bakery was advertising them for sale as early as 1947. Different food entirely from a pizza style, but a genuine, well-documented Erie original worth seeking out on the same trip.
