Bag 1–6
Akro-MilsUS
Drawer Cabinets
Most-cited US brand · "the workhorse"
Flagship: 44-Drawer Cabinet (#10144), ≈20"W×6"D×15.8"H, stackable, wall-mountable. $60–95 depending on retailer.
"Reasonably well made" — more ergonomic for micro-categories than tackle boxes.
Harder to find outside the US; UK fans point to Raaco as the closest equivalent.
Source: Rebrickable review + forum thread
IRIS USAUS
Rolling Cart & Mini-Drawers
Preferred over Akro-Mils by at least one detailed guide
Flagship: Rolling Cart w/ Organizer Top, ≈$40 for a 6-drawer unit (May 2023 pricing).
"The indisputable king of my LEGO room."
44-Drawer cabinet: 4.7/5 across 135+ Amazon reviews, LEGO sorting specifically cited. Mini-drawer units reported more stable than Akro-Mils equivalents ($40–50/unit).
Source: BrickNerd guide; Amazon listing
Really Useful BoxesUK/EU
Scrapbook Drawers Tower
Near-ubiquitous among UK AFOLs
Drawers sized for 12×12" scrapbook paper; optional 15-compartment Hobby Tray insert, ≈10.5×7.0×7.5 cm/compartment (≈0.55L) — about 70% larger than purpose-built Papimax LEGO drawers.
Towers: ≈$23 (single 9.5L drawer) to ≈$120 (8-drawer tower); replacement drawers ≈$20 each.
Source: BrickArchitect large-collection guide; Really Useful Products site; Brickset review (secondhand — see sourcing notes)
Craft & Hobby OrganizersCrossover
ArtBin · Stanley · Plano
Legitimate, community-used alternatives, not fringe
ArtBin Super Satchel — clear polypropylene, ≈15.2×8×6.2", removable dividers, stackable. Popular enough that one builder (Adam Savage, documented) built a custom cabinet just to house stacks of them.
Stanley Removable Compartment Pro (≈$15, 25 compartments) and Plano 3700-series tackle boxes (up to 24 compartments) work, but tackle-box layouts make finding a specific part slower.
Source: BrickArchitect storage-ideas page; large-collection guide
Official LEGO BricksRoom Copenhagen
Storage Brick Drawers
Mixed verdict
2×2 (1 drawer) ≈$25; 2×4 (2 drawers) ≈$35.
Pro: stacks like a real brick; enclosed drawers don't spill in transit; drawer front accepts an actual LEGO plate for labeling.
Con: less sturdy than a solid brick; no 1×2 size; costs more per unit than generic cabinets; some reported discoloration on white units.
Verdict: better for bulk/thematic storage than granular single-part sorting.
Source: TheBrickBlogger review

What To Weigh
Constraint
Drawer Size & Depth

See the spec drawing above for the concrete numbers. Favor shallow over deep across the board — buried pieces are hard to spot.

Near-Mandatory
Stackability

IRIS and Akro-Mils cabinets both use interlocking lid grooves/tabs specifically so units stack — called a core selection principle across multiple guides.

Built-In or DIY
Labeling

BrickArchitect provides printable label sheets for exactly this purpose. Official LEGO storage bricks build labeling in — the drawer front accepts an actual LEGO plate.

Wide Range
Cost per Drawer

≈$0.50–$1/compartment-equivalent for bulk Sterilite/Akro-Mils, up to ≈$20/drawer for Really Useful, ≈$25–35/unit for official LEGO bricks.

Lead, Not Confirmed
Durability

One search-summary source describes IRIS plastic as more rigid/stable than Sterilite's — not independently verified. Treat as a lead.

Near-Universal
Clear vs. Opaque

Clear/translucent (IRIS, Sterilite ClearView, ArtBin) is favored across nearly every source — see contents without opening. No source argued for opaque.

Thresholds per BrickArchitect. "Element" = part + color, LEGO's own warehouse practice — recommended for personal collections only at the top tier, since labor and drawer count scale enormously. Sorting by part alone is called "sufficient for most large collections, because it is easy for most people to distinguish between colors."
Where AFOLs Genuinely Disagree
BrickArchitect

Flags pure color-sorting — most people's instinctive first step — as a dead end once you need to find a specific part, not just admire a pile of one color. Recommends progressing up the category→part→element ladder as collections grow.

BrickNerd

Pushes back on fine-grained sorting as an ideal: heavy reliance on small/mini drawers "wastes space and money" and creates unnecessarily long sorting sessions "without proportional benefit." Favors broad categories and larger uniform containers even at big collection sizes.

Not a clean binary of "by element" vs. "by category" — a genuine disagreement among experienced AFOLs about how far up the granularity ladder is actually worth the labor.
