MOC
"My Own Creation" — the umbrella term for any original, non-instruction build. Habitats are one sub-genre of MOC.
Vignette
The smallest scene format in the size hierarchy.
Bignette
Larger than a vignette, smaller than a diorama.
Diorama
The largest scene format in the hierarchy.
Habitat
Not really a size category — a themed, enclosed environment built for one minifigure, usually at 8×8.
MILS
Modular Integrated Landscaping System — 32×32 Technic-pinned baseplates for collaborative convention-floor terrain. Related idea, different (larger) genre — don't conflate the two.
The Standard Format
Read left to right: three 8×8 habitat modules interlocking edge to edge via alternating single-stud overhangs — the mechanism that lets independently-built habitats stack into a wall at a meetup or on a shelf.

Five Design Principles
From Josh Parkinson's BrickNerd piece, "Skillful Habitats: The Five Keys To Building Small" — the closest thing the genre has to a shared rulebook.
🎨
Color
Restrict to 2–3 dominant colors, with grey/black/white as permitted neutrals. Shade variation within the palette creates depth — not new hues.
🎯
Focus
Clean, uncluttered composition. "Keep it simple, AFOL" — the minifigure or central subject stays undistracted by competing detail.
🔍
Texture
At 8×8, every stud counts. Plates, minifig accessories, printed/stickered tiles, and SNOT techniques deliver detail without eating disproportionate space.
🗺
Composition
"Clump and contrast" — group detail into distinct clumps rather than scattering it evenly; use color/light/texture contrast to direct the eye.
📖
Story
Favor a captured narrative moment — mid-action, turned away, interacting with another figure — over a static pose. Built-in lighting often reinforces it.

“The more you can hone your color palette, the easier it will be to create a striking creation.”

Josh Parkinson (BrickBuiltJosh), BrickNerd
Advanced Technique — Integrated Stacks
From BrickNerd's "Integrated Habitats: The Art of the Seamless Stack" — the progression beyond a plain single-stud interlock, toward "it can be hard to tell if it's even a stack of habitats anymore."
1
Framing / Consistency
Shared color palette and window styling carried across every module in the stack.
2
Integrated New Look
A unified thematic architecture ties the modules together as one building, not just adjacent scenes.
3
Blurred Edges
Extensions, irregular shapes, and landscape elements bridge the seams between habitats.
4
Radical Reorientation
Interiors built alongside exteriors; the standard stacking orientation gets rotated entirely.
5
360° Designs
Interior and exterior displayed simultaneously — the stack becomes a fully walkaround piece.
Turntable bases Technic pins Clip connectors + central risers Wedge-shaped bridging pieces Anti-studs for paneling Overhanging plates/tiles to hide the base outline

LEGO Group's Relationship to the Trend
No official, ratified LEGO Group habitat product line exists — this stays fan-driven. But two notable, unequal-weight crossovers are worth flagging.
LEGO Party! (video game) Verified
SMG Studio × The LEGO Group · shipped Sep 30, 2025 · PS5/PS4/Xbox/Switch/PC
Built in direct collaboration with LEGO Group, using only "legal build" brick constructions throughout. Its achievement icons are explicitly styled after the habitat format — 8×8, isometric dioramas — mostly designed by Lewis Meeny (@built_bricks). The clearest documented case of the format crossing into an official LEGO Group-collaborated product.
“The habitat format is something I always loved seeing at various LEGO shows as it's a format that distills down the builds to the core creative use of parts.” — Ashley Ringrose, SMG Studio founder
LEGO CMF Series 27 tie-in (BrickNerd) Verified
Dave Schefcik, BrickNerd · featuring builder ABrickDreamer (Davide Sacramati)
LEGO Group supplied product to BrickNerd for this coverage, which happened to take habitat form as the AFOL media outlet's own creative choice — not an official LEGO Group statement on the genre itself. Worth including, but weighted lighter than LEGO Party! above.
