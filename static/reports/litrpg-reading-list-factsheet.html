System Window — Genre LineageRead top to bottom
Reading this diagram: Wikipedia's own progression-fantasy article states LitRPG "has been described both as a sub-genre of progression fantasy and as its cousin" — the dashed red line marks that this boundary is genuinely unsettled, not a judgment call made here. "LitRPG" was coined in Russia in late 2013; "progression fantasy" was coined in 2019 by Andrew Rowe (Arcane Ascension) and Will Wight (Cradle), with the phrase itself suggested by a reader, Jess Richards — Rowe traces one root back to Quag Keep (1979, Andre Norton), an early D&D-mechanics novel.

Codex Entries
Broader genre-canon landmarks and the background lore behind the reading list — where the genre came from, how it gets published, and what's changing right now.
Origin
LitRPG's Russian Roots
Traces to Russian web fiction; officially recognized in Russia in 2012, with "LitRPG" itself coined late 2013 by writer Vasily Mahanenko, editor Dmitry Malkin, and author/editor Alex Bobl. Play to Live: AlterWorld (Dmitry Rus) and Mahanenko's own Way of the Shaman are the defining early works, translated to English starting ~2014. Most English-language info on this traces to publisher framing (Magic Dome Books) rather than independent literary scholarship — industry lore, not independently verified.
Origin
"Progression Fantasy," Coined 2019
Andrew Rowe (Arcane Ascension) and Will Wight (Cradle) coined the term because their books resembled LitRPG and xianxia but lacked LitRPG's literal interfaces or xianxia's cultural trappings — phrase suggested by a reader, Jess Richards. Rowe traces one tributary back to Quag Keep (1979, Andre Norton), one of the earliest novels built on D&D-style mechanics.
Economy
Royal Road → Print Pipeline
Royal Road (founded 2011, originally a Korean web-novel fan-translation site) is now the central self-publishing hub for LitRPG/progression fantasy/isekai — 50M+ monthly visits, functioning as the farm system behind nearly every "started free online, later got a print deal" title in this guide.
Economy
Kindle Unlimited's Fingerprints
KU subscription economics reward binge-readable, long-running series with frequent releases — described as a major structural driver shaping LitRPG's characteristic pacing and volume output.
Landmark
Dungeon Crawler Carl, By the Numbers
8 books, 14M+ copies combined, 140M+ Audible listening hours, first book alone 2.7M+ copies. Ace Books (Penguin Random House) acquired print rights in 2024 — all 8 now in print. A Peacock TV adaptation (Seth MacFarlane's Fuzzy Door + Universal) is confirmed as of mid-2026, with Jeff Hays cast to voice Princess Donut; no premiere date yet. Identified as the tipping point that pulled LitRPG onto mainstream bookstore shelves in the early 2020s.
Breaking
The Wandering Inn Goes to Print
pirateaba's The Wandering Inn — arguably the longest-running, most acclaimed LitRPG web serial ever — was ebook/audiobook/free-web-serial only, until HarperCollins/Harper Voyager signed a deal (~2 years of negotiation), announced March 2026. Book One arrives August 11, 2026. Volumes 1–2 are being pulled back from free web access going forward (audiobooks unaffected). Directly relevant given your physical-first preference — treat as "coming very soon," not "buy now."
Publisher Guild
Who's Actually Printing This Stuff
Aethon Books (imprint Aethon: Vault, distributed by Simon & Schuster) — the genre's dominant commercial publisher (HWFWM, Primal Hunter, Defiance of the Fall); began moving previously ebook-only titles into retail hardcover/paperback in 2025. Mountaindale Press — Dakota Krout's catalog. Wraithmarked Creative — Kickstarter-funded deluxe/signed editions (Iron Prince, Mother of Learning); availability outside active campaign windows can be inconsistent. Ace Books (Penguin Random House) — Dungeon Crawler Carl.

Active Effects — Sourcing Confidence
Debuff · Contested Boundary
Wikipedia's own progression-fantasy article states LitRPG "has been described both as a sub-genre of progression fantasy and as its cousin" — not settled terminology. This sheet treats them as overlapping neighbors rather than picking a side.
Debuff · Unconfirmed Subgenre
"Arena/Gladiator LitRPG" is not named as a standalone top-level subgenre with its own canon by any source found. It reads as a recurring structural device inside tower-climbing and system-apocalypse stories, not a confirmed community category — shown locked/dashed in the Skill Tree above.
Fix Applied · Corrected Attribution
Everybody Loves Large Chests is by Neven Iliev — not "Rhaegar," as an earlier framing assumed. Rhaegar is the author of Azarinth Healer, a different book (listed in the Loot Table above). Corrected throughout this sheet.
