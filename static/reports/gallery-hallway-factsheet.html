Reading this sheet: the width comparison (top) shows why "gallery" and "hallway" feel like different words for different things — a code-minimum hallway and a true long gallery are roughly an order of magnitude apart in width, which is exactly why no single code-defined term covers both. The plan comparison (bottom) is the more useful architectural fact for renovation planning: a double-loaded corridor (rooms both sides) is structurally prone to a dark middle with no daylight source, while a single-loaded corridor (rooms one side, glazing/art wall opposite) is the layout that naturally supports gallery treatment — it's the historical long-gallery pattern at any scale. Terminology
Taxonomy
Gallery Corridor✓ This one
Gallery Corridor
Interior · art wall + glazed wall
An enclosed interior passage, wide enough to furnish and linger in, that carries a display wall opposite a window/view wall. The modern descendant of the long gallery.
This is what Tom is describing
Loggia✕ Different thing
Loggia
Exterior · open on ≥1 side, columned
A covered, column-supported gallery open on at least one side, sharing the main building's roofline but accessed only from inside. Mediterranean/Renaissance origin — a transitional indoor/outdoor space, not an enclosed art corridor. [10]
Redlined out — wrong room type
Breezeway✕ Different thing
Breezeway
Exterior · open-sided connector
A roofed, open-sided passage connecting two separate structures (or two halves of a house), with no walls, windows, or conditioning. Simpler and more casual than a loggia — a connector, not a display space. [11][12]

Planning Considerations
Biggest Lever
Daylighting

Go single-loaded (rooms one side, glazing/view opposite) or layer in a skylight/clerestory as in the Buffalo renovation — double-loaded plans are structurally prone to a dark middle. [3][18]

Not Yet Confirmed
Footprint Cost

No verified industry rule of thumb for the square-footage tradeoff exists — going 36–48" to 54–72" roughly doubles the corridor's footprint for a given length, per the numbers above, but this wasn't independently costed.

Decide Early
Lighting Circuits

Plan track or dedicated picture-light circuits (fixtures 7–10 cm above frame, angled 30–35°) from schematic design — they need their own switching/dimming, not a retrofit. [22][25]

Framing-Stage Decision
Wall Blocking

For heavier/larger art, put continuous plywood blocking behind drywall at the hanging zone during framing — not a post-construction anchor fix. [23][24]

Genuine Gap
Flooring

No gallery-corridor-specific flooring guidance was found beyond general hallway durability advice — flagged as a real gap, not an oversight.

Baseline, Not Target
Code Minimum

36" (IRC) is the floor for any hallway. A true gallery corridor should be planned well above it from the earliest schematic — retrofitting width later means moving structural walls. [8]
