The Published Catalog

Every Cosmere book in print or announced, by series, as of September 2026. Text-confirmed

  • The Emperor's Soul — novella (2012, Tachyon). Also collected in Arcanum Unbounded; easy to find standalone.

Mistborn Era 1 (Scadrial)

  • The Final Empire (2006) · The Well of Ascension (2007) · The Hero of Ages (2008)

Mistborn Era 2 — Wax and Wayne

  • The Alloy of Law (2011) · Shadows of Self (2015) · The Bands of Mourning (2016) · The Lost Metal (2022)

Warbreaker (Nalthis)

  • Standalone (2009).

The Stormlight Archive (Roshar) — Arc 1 of 2, complete

  • The Way of Kings (2010) · Words of Radiance (2014) · Oathbringer (2017) · Rhythm of War (2020) · Wind and Truth (Dec 6, 2024 — closes Arc 1)

Secret Projects (2022 Kickstarter — highest-funded Kickstarter of all time)

  • Tress of the Emerald Sea (2023) · Yumi and the Nightmare Painter (2023) · The Sunlit Man (2023)
  • The Frugal Wizard's Handbook for Surviving Medieval England was part of the same Kickstarter batch but is not Cosmere — easy to assume otherwise.

White Sand (Taldain)

Graphic novel trilogy, Dynamite (2016 / 2018 / 2019), omnibus 2022 with new prologue. The original prose novel was never published — only a partial prose excerpt exists, inside Arcanum Unbounded. The full story is graphic-novel-only.

Isles of the Emberdark

Expands the 2014 novella Sixth of the Dusk. Dragonsteel-direct hardcover released July 1, 2025; Tor trade hardcover follows February 3, 2026.

Arcanum Unbounded: The Cosmere Collection (2016)

Contents: "The Hope of Elantris"; "The Eleventh Metal"; "The Emperor's Soul"; "Allomancer Jak and the Pits of Eltania, Episodes 28–30"; "White Sand" (prose excerpt); "Shadows for Silence in the Forests of Hell" (Threnody); "Sixth of the Dusk" (First of the Sun); "Mistborn: Secret History"; "Edgedancer" (Stormlight, Lift).

Print-availability flags — Mistborn: Secret History's primary print home is Arcanum Unbounded (later standalone reprints exist per bookseller listings). Sixth of the Dusk has no standalone print edition at all — print-only inside Arcanum Unbounded, though a standalone ebook exists; now superseded in scope by Isles of the Emberdark. "The Hope of Elantris," "The Eleventh Metal," and "Allomancer Jak" are print-only inside Arcanum Unbounded — some began as free online serials.

Announced, not yet published (as of Sept 2026)

  • The Fires of December — "Secret Project Six," first of Hoid's Travails (first-person Hoid, lesser-known worlds). Crowdfund edition earlier; commercial Tor edition targeted Dec 2026. Confirmed
  • Ghostbloods (Mistborn Era 3) — book 1 "mostly done." Sanderson: "2028 is a pretty firm release date... don't expect it to move." Projected Dec 2028 / 2029 / 2030 for the trilogy. Confirmed
  • Stormlight Book 6 (Arc 2 opener) — no firm date. Sanderson says only that he feels "the call to return," likely "in the next few years," complicated by TV commitments. A circulating "2031" figure is fan extrapolation, not his words
  • Hoid's Storybook Collection crowdfund — March 2026. Confirmed

Note: Blightfall (Riftwake) is not Cosmere and not written by Sanderson — excluded here by standing rule.

SECTION 02

Reading Orders — The Competing Schools

There is no consensus reading order, and none of what follows is ranked against the others — presenting one as "best" would misrepresent the research. Text-confirmed

Sanderson's own guidance (faq.brandonsanderson.com): "it really doesn't matter which Cosmere series you start with." Five valid entry points: Mistborn: The Final Empire, The Emperor's Soul, The Way of Kings, Elantris, Warbreaker. His one hard rule: don't skip ahead within a series. He flags The Alloy of Law as spoiling Mistborn Era 1, so it's a weak entry point despite opening Era 2.

Sanderson's official chronological order

Elantris → The Emperor's Soul → Mistborn Era 1 → Warbreaker → Shadows for Silence → Stormlight → Mistborn Era 2 → Sixth of the Dusk → future Mistborn — with his own caveat that several placements are unconfirmed ("RAFO"). Text-confirmed, with acknowledged gaps

Fan-constructed orders

Publication order and various "connections-first" hybrids disagree with each other on details — notably where Warbreaker sits relative to Words of Radiance, and where the Secret Projects land. What each optimizes for:

  • Publication order — spoiler-safest, reads as released.
  • Chronological — in-world sequence, but demands more Cosmere literacy up front.
  • Connections-first — catches cameos and Realmatic tie-ins early, at the cost of reading harder books sooner. The recurring version of this pattern: Mistborn Era 1 → Warbreaker → Stormlight → Mistborn Era 2 → Secret Projects.

Fan-derived — none of these three is endorsed by Sanderson

SECTION 03

Adonalsium and the Shattering

Adonalsium was the power of creation. In the Shattering, a group of sixteen individuals killed it; each took a portion and became a Shard. Every Shard carries an Intent that colors both its Vessel's personality and the magic system it produces on whatever world it settles. Text-confirmed

This is the single fact that makes the Cosmere one universe rather than several unrelated fantasy worlds: every magic system in every series is a local expression of the same shattered power, filtered through whichever Shard's Investiture dominates that world.

⚠Spoiler — Rhythm of War (2020)

In Rhythm of War, Taravangian kills Rayse — the original Vessel of Odium — and ascends to hold the Shard himself. He later renames it Retribution. This single event reframes the political endgame of the back half of Stormlight Arc 1 and is the one piece of "settled Cosmere lore" in this report that genuinely turns on a late-book revelation.

SECTION 05

Realmatic Theory and Investiture

Everything in the Cosmere exists in three Realms simultaneously: Physical (matter), Cognitive (perception and mind — how a thing is viewed, including by itself), and Spiritual (essence and connections). Text-confirmed

Investiture is the shared substrate: power tied to a specific Shard, which cannot be created or destroyed and flows from higher to lower concentration. It underlies every magic system — Allomancy, Surgebinding, Awakening — collectively the Invested Arts. Initiation into a magic system generally requires a "crack" in a person's spiritweb for Investiture to bond into. Text-confirmed

SECTION 06

Worldhopping and Perpendicularities

Where a Shard's Investiture pools heavily, it can form a perpendicularity — a point where the three Realms align enough that someone can slide between them, and from there, between planets. Text-confirmed, core concept

No single canonical explainer of the precise mechanics exists — this account is synthesized from Coppermind's Realm pages, not drawn from one authoritative passage.

SECTION 07

Hoid Across the Books

Hoid appears in some form in every published Cosmere book — confirmed by Sanderson himself. Confirmed

  • Aliases across the catalog: Wit (Stormlight Archive), a wandering storyteller (Warbreaker), Cephandrius / Dust / Wanderer (Stormlight epigraph letters), Hoid (Mistborn).
  • Said to originate on Yolen; can manipulate Fortune, sensing where he needs to be without knowing why.

Where the line actually sits. Confirmed: the cameos themselves — he is identifiable on the page across the catalog. Not confirmed: his full backstory and origin. Sanderson has explicitly reserved that for the unwritten Dragonsteel series. Claims that "Hoid was present at the Shattering," or that he predates the Shards, are fan synthesis pulled from convention Q&As and Words of Brandon — not stated in any published novel. This is the single most-blurred fact in casual Cosmere discussion online, which is exactly why it gets called out here rather than folded into the confirmed list above.

Cameos: Confirmed   Origin / Shattering presence: Fan-derived, not in text

The Fires of December (Hoid's Travails) is written first-person as Hoid and may resolve some of this once published — it hasn't yet, as of this report.

SECTION 08

Where the Story Stands

Stormlight
Ten books, two five-book arcs — confirmed directly by Sanderson. Arc 1 is complete with Wind and Truth, which he describes as "a distinct ending for this sequence." Arc 2 (books 6–10) shifts focus toward Jasnah, Lift, and Renarin. He plans a multi-year break and is doing Mistborn Era 3 first.
Mistborn
A planned four-era structure running from Era 1's renaissance-level technology toward a Word-of-Brandon space-opera finale with Allomancy-powered spaceships — this describes his pitch to his editor, not yet on the page. Era 3 (Ghostbloods) book 1 is mostly done; Dec 2028 is stated as firm.
The endgame
Sanderson has spoken of roughly 30–40 total Cosmere novels (often cited as ~35). The exact figure could not be pinned to one quotable statement — treat it as order-of-magnitude, not a count. Dragonsteel is intended to tell the Shattering/Hoid backstory and serve as connective tissue for a multi-series convergence.
Published vs. plan
5 of 10 Stormlight. 7 of ~13 Mistborn (3 + 4, with Era 3's three still to come and an unconfirmed Era 4 beyond that). Elantris: 1 of 3. Dragonsteel — the series meant to anchor the endgame — is entirely unpublished. That's worth sitting with: the thing that's supposed to explain how everything connects doesn't exist on the page yet.
Next, concretely
Isles of the Emberdark Tor trade (Feb 2026) → Hoid's Storybook Collection crowdfund (Mar 2026) → The Fires of December (Dec 2026) → Ghostbloods (Dec 2028, 2029, 2030) → Stormlight Arc 2, undated.

Entry Points

  • New to fantasy: Elantris (or Steelheart, non-Cosmere) — Sanderson's own suggestion. Confirmed
  • Experienced fantasy reader: Mistborn: The Final Empire or The Way of Kings — his top two picks. Mistborn is the lighter commitment (~650pp vs. Stormlight's far larger scope). Confirmed
  • Wants the connected-universe payoff fastest: no official answer exists. The recurring fan pattern is Mistborn Era 1 → Warbreaker → Stormlight → Mistborn Era 2 → Secret Projects, on the logic that later books assume cumulative Cosmere literacy. Fan-constructed
SECTION 10

For the Collector

The direct-first pattern — the actionable finding

Secret Projects went to backers and Dragonsteel-direct first (Tress: backers Jan 1 2023, general Jan 10), with Tor's commercial trade edition arriving roughly three months later (Tress: Apr 4 2023). The same pattern held for Isles of the Emberdark (direct Jul 2025 → Tor Feb 2026), and is likely for The Fires of December (crowdfund Mar 2026 → commercial Dec 2026 — the "likely" is an inference from the established pattern, not a confirmed date match). Unique cover art, formats, and first-print variants live on the Dragonsteel side, and for some editions direct is the only route at all.

This is the same shape as the Subterranean Press / Bujold situation already in your author tracker — a publisher-direct channel that carries exclusive editions and first dibs on signed stock, running ahead of and separate from the mainline trade release. Treat Dragonsteel-direct the same way: check it specifically, don't assume the Tor edition is the only or the first option.

What the leatherbound editions physically are

The Warbreaker 10th Anniversary leatherbound is the template: bonded leather, Smyth-sewn (not glued) binding, gilded page edges, two-color foiling, a four-page art gallery, new commissioned interior illustrations, and endpapers by original cover artist Dan dos Santos. Announced for Q4 2026: an illustrated Skyward leatherbound with 30+ new interiors by Jake Parker. The Lost Metal leatherbound carries new endpages and art by Bryan Mark Taylor and Victor Adame Minguez. Confirmed, dragonsteelbooks.com + Dan dos Santos's own account of the Warbreaker edition
