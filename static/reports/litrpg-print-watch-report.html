§ 01

Scope, Method & How to Read This Report

This report is the first run (Cycle 01) of a new recurring weekly task tracking newly available physical print LitRPG releases — paperback and hardcover only. Ebook-only and audio-only releases are explicitly out of scope for this tracking effort, regardless of how notable they are, because Tom's stated interest here is specifically physical availability.

This cycle's window covers roughly August 4–25, 2026. It is headlined by a genuinely landmark event for the genre — the first-ever print edition of The Wandering Inn — alongside five other confirmed new physical titles, one partially confirmed release, and three items that could not be confirmed this cycle and are carried forward as open leads rather than presented as findings.

A methodological point worth stating plainly, since it shapes every entry below: physical availability was verified directly against publisher and retailer product pages (Aethon Books' own book pages, Target, Walmart, Amazon, McNally Robinson) rather than inferred from release announcements alone. This distinction matters specifically in LitRPG, a genre where a title can be announced, "available," or even actively selling in ebook/Kindle Unlimited form for months or years before — or entirely without ever — a print edition materializing. An announcement is a lead; a live product page with a paperback or hardcover SKU is a confirmation.

Four confidence categories are used consistently throughout this report:

ConfirmedVerified directly against a live publisher or retailer product page showing a physical (paperback/hardcover) SKU, with a release date.
Partially confirmedA genuine print SKU exists and was located, but a specific detail — typically exact release date or price — could not be independently verified this cycle.
Flagged / could not confirmA release is claimed somewhere, but no product page confirming physical format, date, or price could be located this cycle. Carried forward as an open item, not reported as a finding.
Coverage gapA publisher or channel that was checked but returned inconclusive results (e.g. stale cached listing pages) — different from "confirmed no releases," and flagged as such rather than silently treated as a null result.

As the first run of this task, there is no prior cycle to diff against. Every title below is reported as "new to this tracker" relative to a zero baseline, not relative to a previous week's list — that comparison becomes possible starting with Cycle 02.

§ 02

The Headline — The Wandering Inn's Print Debut

The Wandering Inn, written by pirateaba, is widely regarded as one of the longest-running and most acclaimed LitRPG web serials in existence — it has been publishing continuously online since July 2016. Until this cycle, it existed only as a free web serial, an ebook, and an audiobook. That changed on August 11, 2026, with the release of Book One, Part One in paperback through HarperCollins — the series' first-ever physical print edition.

Authorpirateaba
PublisherHarperCollins

Web serial sinceJuly 2016 — roughly a decade of free/ebook-only availability preceding this print debut
Edition notePer the author's own announcement, this is a rewritten and re-edited edition prepared specifically for the print deal — not a straight reprint of the existing web text.

The four-part rollout

HarperCollins is publishing the print edition in four installments on a monthly cadence running from August through November 2026:

InstallmentRelease DateStatus as of Aug 29, 2026
Book One, Part OneAug 11, 2026Released
Book One, Part TwoSept 22, 2026Upcoming
Book Two, Part OneOct 20, 2026Upcoming
Book Two, Part TwoNov 10, 2026Upcoming

Only Part One has actually released as of this report's compile date; the remaining three installments are tracked commitments for future cycles of this task, not releases to report yet.

A notable change for existing free-serial readers

As part of the traditional-publishing tradeoff behind this deal, Volumes 1–2 of the web serial are now gated behind the print/ebook release rather than being freely available on the web serial site, as they had been for roughly a decade. This is a real, material change in access for readers who have been following the free version — worth surfacing plainly rather than only celebrating the print milestone.

The author's own announcement post is dated March 28, 2026 (per the post's own URL), several months ahead of the August release — consistent with a deal of this scale requiring lead time to announce, produce, and distribute before the first installment reaches shelves.

This finding is confirmed via four independent sources: the author's own site (wanderinginn.com), a ScreenRant article covering the release specifically, and two live retailer paperback listings — Walmart and McNally Robinson — both showing an active paperback SKU distinct from any ebook listing.

§ 04

Partially Confirmed — Apex Guardian

Apex Guardian (Paladin of the Forsaken Lands, Book 1), by Jollybane, published by Aethon Books, sits in the partially-confirmed tier for Cycle 01. This is stated as a deliberate, specific caveat rather than folded into the confirmed list, because two different things are true at once here:

What Is Confirmed vs. What Is Not
ConfirmedA genuine paperback product listing exists on Amazon — ISBN 9798192371176, ASIN B0HF1V6BB6 — distinct from the separate Kindle edition. Aethon's own August 2026 release-slate announcement places this series' Book 1 on August 18, 2026.
Not confirmedThe Amazon page's price and ship-date details could not be pulled directly this cycle, and no dedicated Aethon book-detail page (of the kind that exists for the five titles in § 03) could be located for this title.

Net assessment: existence of a print edition is confirmed; the exact release date and price are not. This is exactly the kind of gap this report is built to surface honestly rather than paper over with a plausible-sounding guess — a specific date or price is not stated here because neither was independently verified.

§ 06

Publisher Coverage Notes

This cycle's confirmed releases lean heavily on Aethon Books (four of the six titles addressed in §§ 02–04) plus one Podium Publishing title and the HarperCollins headline. That concentration is partly a reflection of Aethon's dominant position in commercial LitRPG publishing, and partly an artifact of which publisher listing pages loaded reliably this cycle.

Five other channels worth watching in this genre — Mountaindale Press, Wraithmarked Creative, Royal Road author-direct storefronts, Tapas Media, and Portal Books — returned inconclusive results this run due to stale cached listing data. This is recorded here as a specific, named gap in this cycle's coverage rather than smoothed into the confirmed-releases list or silently dropped. A goal for Cycle 02 is closing this gap with fresh checks against each of these five channels.

§ 08

Tracking Methodology Going Forward

This report establishes the baseline for a recurring weekly task. The intended cadence and standards going forward:

Standing Rules for Future Cycles
Scope stays locked to physical print

Paperback and hardcover releases only. Ebook- and audio-only titles remain out of scope regardless of how notable they are, unless the household explicitly changes this instruction.

Verification standard stays retailer/publisher-first

A title is "confirmed" only once a live publisher or retailer product page shows a physical SKU. A release announcement alone is a lead, not a confirmation, given how commonly ebook and print availability diverge in this genre.

Diff against the prior cycle starting Cycle 02

Future reports should explicitly note which confirmed titles are newly appearing versus already known from a previous cycle (e.g. Wandering Inn Book One Pt. 2 becoming "confirmed released" rather than "upcoming" once Sept 22, 2026 arrives), rather than re-presenting the full history each time.

Coverage gaps get named, not absorbed

When a publisher or channel returns inconclusive data (as happened this cycle with five publishers, § 06), that should continue to be reported explicitly as a gap to close, rather than treated as an implicit "no releases" finding.

No sources/citations apparatus beyond what follows is needed for future weekly runs to stay lightweight, but the same tiered sourcing discipline used here — distinguishing a publisher's own product page from a retailer listing from an editorial/news writeup — should carry forward each cycle.
