§ 01

How This Log Works

This page tracks newly available physical print LitRPG releases — paperback and hardcover only — plus a small set of non-LitRPG authors Tom follows the same way (Weber, Fforde, Bujold, Pierce, Sanderson, Tchaikovsky). Ebook-only and audio-only releases are explicitly out of scope regardless of how notable they are, because Tom's stated interest here is specifically physical availability.

The weekly print-release-check task runs every Saturday. Earlier, a report was only published when a run turned up something "genuinely meaty" — three runs (Sept 5, 12, 19) did real research whose findings only ever landed in a scheduled-session transcript. That was the wrong tradeoff: it made this page look abandoned even though the task kept working. As of this restructuring, every run gets an entry below, including a run that finds nothing — a quiet week is a legitimate, expected outcome, not a gap in the log.

A methodological point that shapes every entry: physical availability is verified directly against publisher and retailer product pages (Aethon's own book pages, Barnes & Noble, Amazon, Walmart, Target, publisher sites) rather than inferred from release announcements alone. This distinction matters specifically in LitRPG, a genre where a title can be announced or actively selling in ebook/Kindle Unlimited form for months or years before — or entirely without ever — a print edition materializing. An announcement is a lead; a live product page with a paperback or hardcover SKU is a confirmation.

Four confidence categories are used consistently throughout this log:

ConfirmedVerified directly against a live publisher or retailer product page showing a physical (paperback/hardcover) SKU, with a release date.
Partially confirmedA genuine print SKU exists and was located, but a specific detail — typically exact release date or price — could not be independently verified that run.
Flagged / could not confirmA release is claimed somewhere, but no product page confirming physical format, date, or price could be located. Carried forward in § 04 as an open item, not reported as a finding.
Coverage gapA publisher or channel that was checked but returned inconclusive results (e.g. a stale cached listing page, a 403/500 error) — different from "confirmed no releases," and tracked explicitly in § 05 rather than silently treated as a null result.

Every run diffs against everything reported in a prior run, so a title is only ever surfaced once. Where a title's status changes between runs (a date appears, a listing is corrected), that is noted as an update rather than re-presented as new.

2026-08-29 — Run 1 (Baseline)1 headline · 5 confirmed · 1 partial · 3 flagged at the time

The Headline — The Wandering Inn's Print Debut

The Wandering Inn, written by pirateaba, is widely regarded as one of the longest-running and most acclaimed LitRPG web serials in existence — it has been publishing continuously online since July 2016. Until this run, it existed only as a free web serial, an ebook, and an audiobook. That changed on August 11, 2026, with the release of Book One, Part One in paperback through HarperCollins — the series' first-ever physical print edition.

Release Dossier
TitleThe Wandering Inn, Book One, Part One
Authorpirateaba
PublisherHarperCollins
FormatPaperback
Release dateAugust 11, 2026
Web serial sinceJuly 2016 — roughly a decade of free/ebook-only availability preceding this print debut
Edition notePer the author's own announcement, this is a rewritten and re-edited edition prepared specifically for the print deal — not a straight reprint of the existing web text.

The four-part rollout

HarperCollins is publishing the print edition in four installments on a monthly cadence running from August through November 2026:

A notable change for existing free-serial readers

As part of the traditional-publishing tradeoff behind this deal, Volumes 1–2 of the web serial are now gated behind the print/ebook release rather than being freely available on the web serial site, as they had been for roughly a decade. A real, material change in access for readers who have been following the free version.

Confirmed via four independent sources: the author's own site (wanderinginn.com), a ScreenRant article covering the release specifically, and two live retailer paperback listings — Walmart and McNally Robinson.

§ 06

Relationship to the Existing Reading List

This log is a companion to the household's existing LitRPG Bookshelf reading list, which is built around Tom's current and completed reads — Dungeon Crawler Carl, He Who Fights with Monsters, and Heretical Fishing — and originally flagged The Wandering Inn's then-upcoming print debut as a "breaking" item. This log is where that item graduated from a forward-looking flag into a confirmed release, and where subsequent developments — HWFWM Book 3 getting a date, the Vault hardcover wave — get tracked concretely as they land.

Where a newly confirmed title plausibly matches Tom's existing taste profile (dungeon-crawler mechanics, RPG-system progression, found-family tone), that judgment is noted inline where it's reported. This log does not re-litigate or expand the reading list's own recommendation tiers; it exists specifically to surface new physical availability on a rolling basis.

§ 07

Standing Rules for This Log

Rules Every Run Follows
Scope stays locked to physical print

Paperback and hardcover releases only. Ebook- and audio-only titles remain out of scope regardless of how notable they are, unless the household explicitly changes this instruction.

Verification standard stays retailer/publisher-first

A title is "confirmed" only once a live publisher or retailer product page shows a physical SKU. A release announcement alone is a lead, not a confirmation.

Every run gets an entry, including quiet ones

A run that finds nothing new still gets logged with a quiet-note entry (§ 03's append-point comment has the exact format). This page stopped being trustworthy when it silently skipped runs — that does not happen again.

Coverage gaps and unreachable sources get named, not absorbed

When a publisher, retailer, or platform returns inconclusive data or fails outright, that is reported explicitly in § 05 rather than treated as an implicit "no releases" finding.

Open items get aged, not re-litigated

§ 04 tracks unresolved leads with an explicit run-count. An item stuck for several runs is a signal worth surfacing on its own, not just quietly re-checked forever.
