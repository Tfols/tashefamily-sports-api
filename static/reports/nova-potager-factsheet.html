The Five Principles
1
Geometric Order
Defined beds and formal patterns make a productive garden read as intentional, not chaotic.
2
Paths as Infrastructure
Gravel or brick paths keep you out of wet soil, define zones, and give the garden bones in winter.
3
A Focal Point
Every composed garden has a visual anchor the eye finds first. Deliberate, not necessarily expensive.
4
Vertical Structure
Arches, trellises, and teepees add the third dimension and improve air circulation in humid summers.
5
Integrated Flowers
Edible and ornamental flowers attract pollinators, deter pests, and fill visual gaps year-round.
NoVA Challenges & Responses
Heavy Clay Soil
Raised beds (10–12 in. amended mix) solve drainage entirely. In-ground needs 2–3 yrs of compost amendment.
Summer Heat & Humidity
Disease-resistant cultivars + good air circulation. Succession plant cool crops; don’t fight July–Aug with greens.
Deer & Rabbits
6-ft fence minimum for deer; 24-in. hardware cloth at base for rabbits. Design it in from the start.
False Spring Trap
Last frost avg. Apr 7–15. Wait for soil ≥55°F before warm-season transplants — early plants stall.
Spotted Lanternfly
Now established in NoVA. Remove Tree of Heaven (primary host). Kill on contact; scrape egg masses.
Irrigation Principles
Drip irrigation is the strong consensus recommendation

Delivers water to the root zone, not foliage — the primary defense against fungal disease in Virginia’s humid summers. Use 30–50% less water than overhead sprinklers for equivalent results (UGA Extension).

Drip + Timer
Best system for raised beds. Set-and-manage; consistent watering prevents stress cycles that invite disease.
Soaker Hose
Budget alternative; works well in raised beds. Less precise than emitters; replace every 2–5 yrs.
Rain Barrel
Legal in Virginia. 50-gal barrel supplements irrigation in shoulder seasons; doesn’t cover July drought.

Never overhead water in the evening. Morning only — foliage must dry before nighttime humidity sets in.

Companion Planting: Evidence Summary
French Marigold
Strong evidence. T. patula reduces root-knot nematodes 40–70%. Plant densely; maintain 8–12 weeks minimum.
Three Sisters
Strong evidence. Corn + beans + squash polyculture: 20–40% yield advantage over monoculture (LER studies).
Sweet Alyssum
Good evidence. Attracts hoverflies and parasitoid wasps; 50–70% aphid reduction adjacent to beds.
Basil + Tomato
Modest evidence. Basil volatile oils reduce thrips on adjacent plants. Does not improve flavor (no biological basis).
Fennel
Documented harmful. Allelopathic root exudates inhibit neighboring plants. Isolate in its own bed or container.
Structures in the Potager
→
Garden Shed
Historically present in functional potagers. Modern approach: design it as a visual feature rather than hiding it. Position at edge with herb beds in front.
→
Cold Frames
Glass-topped boxes extend the fall season by 4–6 weeks. Same footprint as a garden bed. Historically appropriate and practically valuable.
→
Arbors & Arches
Both focal point and growing structure. Grow beans, cucumbers, or small gourds — crops productive enough to justify the structure.
→
Greenhouse / Pavilion
Victorian addition to the tradition, but historically a garden pavilion or viewing house has always been part of formal potager design (Jefferson’s Monticello brick pavilion).
→
Materials Rule
Weather-natural materials (wood, bamboo, wrought iron) age into the garden. Powder-coated metal ages poorly. A cedar arch silvers to match cedar beds over time.
